Food Lens AI was a large scale production food-recognition and nutrition platform. I owned the backend and ML infrastructure behind the system, operating at **50+ requests per second with &lt;300 ms inference latency**.

Pipeline cleanup of 20M+ images, 1M+ indexed products, 50+ req/s , &lt; 300 ms inference latency

The interesting challenge was not simply improving the recognition model. It was building the data pipelines, retrieval architecture, evaluation systems, and production infrastructure required to make computer-vision-based food recognition work reliably at scale.


## Packaged-Goods Recognition

Food-on-a-plate recognition and packaged-product recognition are fundamentally different problems. The existing CNN-based system worked well for visual food categories, but packaged goods have enormous variation and often need to resolve to a specific commercial product and its corresponding nutritional data.

There was also no usable packaged-goods image corpus at the scale we needed.

I built the pipeline end-to-end, starting with **20M+ product images** collected through large-scale scraping and ingestion pipelines. Images went through **OpenCV-based preprocessing, deduplication, and automated quality checks**, reducing the raw corpus to over **1M production-ready products**.  

Barcode and QR extraction connected recognized products with structured nutritional databases. For cases that could not be resolved automatically, I built an **OCR and LLM-assisted administration workflow** for extracting, retrieving, and validating product labels and nutritional information. 

Millions of **Transformer-based embeddings** were generated using GPU-enabled Apache Beam/Dataflow workers and used to power the production retrieval system.

<ProjectDetailSection title="Low-Latency Vector Retrieval">

Serving millions of embeddings with production latency requirements presented a separate engineering problem.

I built a **two-tier retrieval architecture**. Frequently queried products were kept in a GPU-resident hot-item index, while **Vertex AI Vector Search** provided retrieval across the complete product corpus.  

For the hot path, I optimized vector search using **compiled PyTorch kernels, precomputed vector norms, and batched L2 distance calculations**, minimizing both computation and data movement during inference. 

This retrieval system ran alongside the existing CNN classifier: classification for food-on-a-plate recognition and embedding-based retrieval for packaged products.

The split was deliberate. Trying to force both problems through the same recognition architecture would have compromised either latency, accuracy, or both.

</ProjectDetailSection>

<ProjectDetailSection title="Nutrition Search & RAG">

Nutrition lookup presented a different retrieval problem.

Traditional keyword search becomes increasingly poor as the food database grows. A query such as “apple” may match hundreds of vendor products and database entries, while the user actually needs meaningful concepts such as red apple, green apple, or Fuji apple.

I built a **RAG-based nutritional search system** over **USDA FoodData Central and NutritionX**. The system combined **dense and sparse retrieval over chunked documents, re-ranking, and LLM generation** to answer detailed macro- and micronutrient questions rather than simply returning raw database matches.

The result was a retrieval layer combining structured nutritional databases, semantic and lexical search, re-ranking, and generative extraction instead of relying on any one technique exclusively.

</ProjectDetailSection>

<ProjectDetailSection title="Evaluation & Production Reliability">

As the corpus continuously expanded and product labels evolved, retrieval quality could regress without any obvious failure in the serving infrastructure.

I built a **regression and observability system around curated golden datasets** to detect changes in retrieval quality and embedding behavior as new food and packaged-goods data entered production. 

This gave us a repeatable way to evaluate changes to embeddings, retrieval logic, and the underlying corpus before they affected production results.

</ProjectDetailSection>

## Production & Integrations

Food Lens AI powered consumer applications and was also exposed as an API for external products.

The technology was integrated with **Samsung Bixby** and the Food Recognition API was licensed to enterprise partners, including **Helix**, where food recognition was used as part of DNA-based meal personalization.

## Technology

**Python, PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, OpenAI, Vertex AI Vector Search, OpenCV, FastAPI, Apache Beam / Dataflow, Kubernetes, Docker, Cloud Run, GPU inference**

## Outcomes

* Scaled packaged-goods recognition from no usable corpus to 3M+ production-ready products, derived from more than 20M collected images.
* Served production food recognition at 20+ req/s with &lt;300 ms inference latency while combining CNN classification and large-scale vector retrieval.
* Expanded Food Lens AI beyond visual classification into product and nutritional retrieval, supporting both packaged goods and detailed macro/micronutrient queries.
* Shipped the technology into consumer and B2B products, including Samsung Bixby and enterprise integrations such as Helix.
* Established regression testing for a continuously evolving ML corpus, allowing new products, labels, embeddings, and retrieval changes to be evaluated against curated golden datasets before production.
