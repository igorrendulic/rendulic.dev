## Overview

I co-founded Amplio in 2009 as a small software company building web, mobile, and interactive products. We grew to a team of five, and as CEO I was responsible for managing the team and projects while remaining deeply involved in engineering — designing systems, building products, working directly with customers, and leading projects from early concepts through production.

The work covered a broad range of products: mobile applications, location-based games, digital publishing and archives, voice applications, and interactive public terminals.

## Engineering & leadership

Running Amplio meant combining engineering with product and project leadership. I worked across product definition, architecture, backend development, mobile and web systems, customer requirements, and delivery, while coordinating a five-person team across multiple concurrent projects.

The projects were smaller than the distributed systems and AI platforms I would work on later, but they required a different kind of breadth: taking loosely defined customer or product ideas, turning them into technical plans, assigning and coordinating the work, and getting working products into users' hands.

That experience became the foundation for the larger engineering teams, distributed systems, data platforms, and AI products I would later build and lead.

## Selected projects & outcomes

### PHAIDRA / E-Theses — EU TEMPUS

Worked on systems developed as part of the EU TEMPUS **New Library Services at Western Balkan Universities** initiative, which modernized university library infrastructure across Serbia, Montenegro, and Bosnia and Herzegovina.

The project established institutional repositories based on PHAIDRA, the digital asset management and long-term archiving system developed at the University of Vienna. 

Amplio developed the **E-Theses** application for the University of Belgrade, providing search and access to doctoral dissertations stored across the PHAIDRA repositories.

The system became part of the regional academic repository infrastructure and remains in operation today.

### EKO Terminals — ZEOS

Led the engineering project with **ZEOS d.o.o.** to give decommissioned Slovenian health-insurance terminals a second life as interactive educational kiosks.

More than 200 former health-card validation terminals were collected. We developed the software architecture and environmental education application, and 80 terminals were converted and deployed to primary schools throughout Slovenia.

Students could explore interactive material about electronic waste and batteries, take quizzes, and use the terminals for their own educational content.

The 80-terminal deployment was completed in less than three months.

### VoiceTask

Built **VoiceTask**, an early Android voice-capture application in 2010.

The application reduced capturing a task or thought to a very small workflow: record it, encode it as MP3, and send it directly to a predefined email address.

It could also automatically begin recording after a phone call, making it possible to capture follow-up notes immediately after conversations.

The original Android application is still preserved in third-party software archives, where Amplio is listed as the developer.

### Izdani računi

Developed a web application for small Slovenian businesses to create and manage invoices without requiring traditional accounting software.

### rBook — Digital Publishing

Worked with **MLI-MEDIJA d.o.o.** on **rBook**, a cloud-based digital publishing platform for distributing magazines and other publications across web and mobile devices.

The project was developed during the early transition from print publications to tablet and smartphone consumption, with the goal of giving publishers a single workflow for preparing and distributing digital editions.

The project subsequently placed third in a regional competition.

## Engineering & leadership

Running Amplio meant combining software engineering with product and project leadership.

I coordinated a five-person team across multiple concurrent projects, translated customer and product requirements into technical plans, made architecture decisions, divided and reviewed engineering work, and remained hands-on in implementation.

The projects crossed unusually different problem domains: video streaming, digital archives, geolocation, mobile applications, publishing, industrial process modeling, public interactive systems, and business software.

That experience became the foundation for the larger distributed systems, data platforms, engineering teams, and AI products I would build and lead later in my career.
